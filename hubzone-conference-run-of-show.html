Panelist Questions

Each panelist owns a pillar. Eric will tee you up by name during the round-robin — answer conversationally and tell stories. The first (orange) question is your opener; the rest are directions Eric may steer.
Donna Freitas🎯 Growth Strategy & Capture
Founder & Principal, DMB & Associates · 40+ years across Fortune 500, Alaska Native Corporations, 8(a), HUBZone & small business · Author, Capture and Proposal Process (CAPP) Guide
  1. Open us up — forty years watching companies try to grow, from Fortune 500 to HUBZone shops. Tell the room about a firm that was winning work but had no process underneath it. What happened to them?
  2. You say "developing processes that become practices." What's the first process a HUBZone firm has to install before it's allowed to chase bigger work?
  3. Most small businesses think capture means writing the proposal. Where does the real work start — and how far ahead of the RFP?
  4. You've built BD teams from scratch and mentored the owners running them. What does a scale-ready BD function look like at a 20-person firm versus a 200-person firm?
  5. Closer: the one discipline a HUBZone firm needs before it's ready to scale.
Calvin J. Mitchell Jr.🏛️ Buyer & Prime Side
Sr. Director, Strategic Acquisition & Engagement, GDIT · Former SES Procurement Executive · NCMA Board Advisor
  1. Give the room the inside view — you've been a federal SES procurement exec and now sit on the prime side at GDIT. How do big buyers actually decide who's ready for bigger work?
  2. Where's the gap between how a small business thinks it's evaluated and how primes/agencies really judge scale-readiness?
  3. What makes a prime want to team with — or invest in — a smaller firm?
  4. One operational thing a HUBZone firm must have in place before chasing larger, more complex contracts?
  5. Closer: the fastest credibility signal a growing firm can build this year.
Joel Adelman💰 Funding & Investment Capital
CEO, Encore Funding · Managing Partner, AdCap Growth Partners · CEO, Transcap
  1. Lead with the Encore story — you've built businesses whose whole job is to be "fuel for growth." What did you see that made you go all-in on funding growing companies?
  2. A HUBZone firm just won bigger than they've ever won. What happens to their cash before the government pays — and what are their real options?
  3. Why can't most growing contractors just lean on their bank to scale?
  4. You also sit on the investment side at AdCap. Receivables finance vs. asset-backed vs. investment capital — how should an owner think about which lever, and when? What makes you back a company?
  5. Closer: when should a business owner talk to a funding partner — at the win, or well before?
