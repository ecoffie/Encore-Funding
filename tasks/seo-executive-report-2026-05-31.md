## Plain-English Glossary (Read This First)

This report uses a few search-marketing terms. Here's what each one means in everyday language — no SEO background needed.

| Term | What it actually means |
|---|---|
| **Impression** | Your page *showed up* in someone's Google search results. They saw it — they may or may not have clicked. |
| **Click-Through Rate (CTR)** | Of the people who *saw* your page in Google, the % who actually **clicked** it. If 100 people saw it and 9 clicked, that's a 9% CTR. Higher = your headline is doing its job. |
| **Ranking / Position** | *Where* you show up in Google's results. "Position 1" is the top result; "page one" means the first 10. Higher position = far more clicks. |
| **Indexed** | Google has **found and filed** your page, so it's eligible to show up in searches. Not indexed = invisible to Google, no matter how good the page is. |
| **Organic traffic** | Visitors who found you through a **free Google search** — not from ads you paid for. |
| **Conversion / Key Event** | A visitor took the **action you want** — here, submitting a funding application. The whole point. |
| **Engagement rate** | The % of visitors who actually *did something* (scrolled, clicked, stayed) instead of bouncing immediately. Higher = better-quality traffic. |
| **Schema** | Hidden labels in your page's code that tell Google "this is an article," "this is a business," etc. — which can earn you richer, more eye-catching search listings. |
| **Sitemap** | A list of all your pages that you hand to Google so it knows what to find and file. |
| **Google Search Console (GSC)** | Google's free tool that shows you exactly *how your site performs in search* — what people searched, where you ranked, who clicked. (Big section on this below.) |

## Google Search Console — The Missing Half of Your Data, and Why It's the Key

Everything in this report is measured in **Google Analytics**, which sees only **half the picture** — what visitors do *after* they land. The other half lives in **Google Search Console (GSC)**, and right now you're flying blind on it. This is the single highest-value access you can grant us.

**Google Analytics vs. GSC — they answer opposite questions:**

| Question | Google Analytics (you have) | GSC (you don't) |
|---|---|---|
| How many people visited, and what they did | ✅ | — |
| **Which Google searches showed your page** | — | ✅ |
| **What position you rank at, and your click-through rate** | — | ✅ |
| Whether Google is actually indexing your pages | — | ✅ |
| Whether Google accepted your sitemap | — | ✅ |
| Real Core Web Vitals + whether your schema earns rich results | partial | ✅ |

**Why it's the key for Encore specifically.** Your organic search on the gov subdomain is tiny — 152 sessions in 90 days. *Why* is unanswerable without GSC, and the cause determines the fix:

- **Coverage problem?** (Google isn't indexing the pages) → a technical fix
- **Ranking problem?** (indexed but buried on page 5) → a content/authority fix
- **Snippet problem?** (ranking fine, but weak titles kill the click) → a 30-minute meta rewrite

These need completely different work — and **GSC tells you which one it is in a single screen.** Without it, any SEO spend is guessing.

**The proof it works:** the entire GovCon Giants case study above — 72 pages indexed, 9.2% CTR, page-one rankings — is measurable *only because we run GSC on our own property.* We watch a page get indexed, see the exact queries it ranks for, and optimize the title to lift CTR. That feedback loop is what turns "publish and hope" into a managed growth engine. We'd run the identical loop for Encore.
